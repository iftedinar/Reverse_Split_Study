Do reverse stock splits trigger a momentum "pop," or are they a warning sign?

I tested this directly with a reproducible event study: 88 reverse-split events across 84 U.S.-listed companies (2024–2025), stratified random sample, fully documented and independently accuracy-checked.

The result runs against the popular retail-trading narrative:

→ Median return is negative at EVERY horizon from the split date through 60 trading days later
→ −3.6% on day 0 → −8.7% at one week → −24.8% at three months
→ Zero of 88 events hit a pre-registered "extreme momentum" threshold
→ The pattern survives outlier-trimming and a repeat-issuer check
→ Trading volume and volatility both rise post-split — but that attention tracks with continued selling, not buying

92% of the sample was already trading below $1 before the split, which points to the likely explanation: a reverse split here is mostly a defensive, listing-compliance move by an already-distressed company, not a bullish catalyst.
